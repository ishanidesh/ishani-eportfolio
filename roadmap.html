Academic and Professional Development

My Academic and Career Plan

I want to pursue a career in chip design. My coursework, research experience, and chosen threads are helping me understand the field and decide where I want to focus. Here is how I plan to build on that foundation.

01 / Coursework

Building My Technical Foundation

My coursework has given me a foundation in the mathematics, science, and programming used in computer engineering. Calculus I and II introduced me to methods for analyzing change and solving mathematical problems. Physics I and II helped me understand mechanics, thermodynamics, electricity, and magnetism. Introduction to Electrical and Computer Engineering gave me an overview of the field, while Psychology offered a different perspective on how people think and learn.

Through Introduction to Programming for ECE and Digital Design, I began learning how to write programs and how digital logic is used to build hardware systems. These courses helped me connect the instructions written in software with the hardware that carries them out. They also gave me practice breaking problems into smaller parts and checking whether my solutions worked as intended.

My current courses in Linear Algebra, Object-Oriented Programming, Discrete Mathematics, and ECE 1100 are expanding that foundation. Linear algebra has introduced me to matrices, transformations, and mathematical modeling. Object-oriented programming is helping me organize larger programs, while discrete mathematics is strengthening my understanding of logic, proofs, and algorithms. ECE 1100 has helped me explore engineering career paths and think more carefully about my academic and professional goals. Together, these courses are preparing me for advanced work in hardware, architecture, and cybersecurity.

02 / Research Experience

Applying What I Learn Through Research

My research experience has given me opportunities to work on problems beyond classroom assignments. As an Embedded Systems Research Intern at Worcester Polytechnic Institute, I evaluated embedded AI workloads on a Raspberry Pi 5 for computer vision applications. I measured latency and system performance to understand how different workloads behaved on the device and how the demands of an application affected its operation.

I also used Analog Discovery 2 and shunt-based sensing to measure voltage, current, and power. This work helped me examine performance alongside energy use, which is relevant to my interest in efficient hardware. Debugging Linux-based camera pipelines and embedded software was another part of the experience. Getting tests to run reliably required me to investigate issues across hardware and software and make the testing process more reproducible.

In a separate project, I developed and evaluated a deep learning model for detecting age-related macular degeneration in retinal medical images. I worked on data preprocessing, image analysis, model training, and quantitative evaluation. This project gave me experience with computer vision and the application of machine learning to healthcare. Together, these experiences have helped me see how algorithms, software, and hardware affect one another. They have also encouraged me to seek more research opportunities where I can develop my hardware skills.

03 / My Threads

Computer Hardware and Cybersecurity

My chosen threads are Computer Hardware and Emerging Architectures and Cybersecurity. Computer Hardware and Emerging Architectures connects directly to my interest in chip design. I want to understand how digital circuits, processors, memory, and other components are designed and how their organization affects the behavior of a computing system.

Through this thread, I plan to build a stronger understanding of computer architecture and digital hardware. I want to learn how engineers make decisions involving performance, power consumption, and design complexity. I also hope to gain practical experience using hardware description languages, simulating designs, and testing whether a system meets its requirements. These skills will help me move from studying how hardware works to designing and evaluating hardware myself.

Cybersecurity will help me approach computing systems from another perspective. Along with understanding how a system is supposed to operate, I want to understand how vulnerabilities arise and how engineers can reduce them. I am interested in exploring the relationship between hardware architecture and security, including how design decisions can affect the protection of information.

Studying these areas together will help me consider security alongside performance and reliability. As I complete more coursework and projects, I want to explore where the two threads overlap and identify the topics I enjoy most. That experience will help me narrow my interests within chip design and choose future projects, research opportunities, and internships.

04 / Future Goals

Preparing for a Career in Chip Design

My long-term goal is to work in chip design. My next steps are to strengthen my understanding of digital circuits and computer architecture and gain more experience designing hardware. I plan to learn a hardware description language such as Verilog and use it in a project that I can develop, simulate, and test.

I would like that project to take me through more than just writing a design. I want to define what the system should do, create tests, investigate unexpected behavior, and explain the decisions I made along the way. Keeping a record of the process will help me evaluate my progress and build a portfolio that shows how I solve technical problems.

I also plan to seek research opportunities, project teams, and internships related to digital hardware, embedded systems, or semiconductor design. Working with other students, researchers, and engineers would give me experience with larger projects and help me understand the different roles involved in developing a chip. I plan to attend career events, speak with professionals, and use what I learn to guide my applications and skill development.

Alongside my technical work, I want to improve my ability to communicate design choices, collaborate with a team, and explain test results clearly. By graduation, I hope to have a stronger understanding of the hardware design process and several projects that demonstrate my skills. I expect my interests to become more specific as I gain experience, and I plan to keep adjusting my goals based on the work I find most engaging.
