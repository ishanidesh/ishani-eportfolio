About Me

I’m Ishani Deshpande, a Computer Engineering student at Georgia Tech with an interest in chip design, embedded systems, and cybersecurity.

My Background

I began my college journey at UMass Amherst before transferring to Georgia Tech. Through coursework in programming, digital design, physics, and mathematics, I became increasingly interested in the relationship between hardware and software.

As an Undergraduate Research Intern in WPI’s Bash Lab, I work with early-exit neural networks on Raspberry Pi 5 platforms. My work includes benchmarking model performance, measuring inference latency, and integrating camera capture and image preprocessing into AI workflows. This experience has helped me understand the practical challenges of running machine learning applications on embedded hardware.

I also gained research experience in the Punzo Lab at UMass Chan Medical School, where I assisted with retinal microscopy image analysis, tissue preparation, and research data organization. In a separate project, I developed and evaluated a deep learning model for identifying age-related macular degeneration in retinal images.

My chosen threads are Computer Hardware and Emerging Architectures and Cybersecurity. I want to build stronger hardware design skills and explore how architectural decisions affect performance, energy use, and security.
